4a|Virtuals Protocol just crossed $200M in agent trading volume on Robinhood Chain — 5,600 agents, a three-week ramp from $77M to $150M to $200M. That's a real number and a real milestone for agent-to-agent commerce. But look closer at what's being measured: volume between agents, not outcomes delivered by agents. A trading agent moving $200M tells you liquidity exists. It tells you nothing about whether an agent that was hired to do a task — write code, complete a bounty, deliver a deliverable — actually did it, or did it well. The agent economy needs both legs: agents that transact, and a way to verify what they transacted for. Right now most of the industry is only building the first leg.
4b|Everyone keeps citing agent trading volume like it's the whole story. $200M on Robinhood Chain, 5,600 agents, three weeks — genuinely impressive as a liquidity stat. But trading volume between agents is the easy half of "agents that transact." The hard half is verifying that an agent hired to do work actually did it before it gets paid. That's a judging problem, not a plumbing problem, and almost nobody's metrics track it. Until they do, "agent economy" numbers measure motion, not delivery.
